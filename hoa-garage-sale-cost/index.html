HOA garage sale cost: the factors that go into the budget

We do not publish a typical total, because the cost changes a lot from one community to the next. Here is every line that goes into the budget, so your board can plan with real numbers.

Request a sale Call (602) 767-0428

By Clark Richardson. Last updated November 10, 2026.

The short answer: an HOA garage sale's cost comes down to the size of the community, signs, printing, any permit fees, optional paid promotion, leftover pickup, and either volunteer time or an organizer's fee. Your city's rules and how many homes take part drive most of the difference. Get local quotes and check with your city before the board sets a budget.

The cost factors

Cost factorWhat drives itWays to keep it down
Community sizeHow many homes and entrances, and how spread out the streets are.Start with the sections most likely to take part and grow next year.
SignsNumber of entrances and busy corners, sign size, and reuse.Leave the date off the main signs and use a date rider so they work every year.
PrintingResident flyers, shopper flyers, and printed maps.Use the newsletter, email list, and portal first, and print only what you need.
Permit feesYour city or county. Some charge per home, some have a community option, some charge nothing.Ask the city early, and tell residents at signup if each home needs its own permit.
Paid promotionWhether you boost posts or buy ads for shoppers. It is optional.Start with free local buy and sell groups, neighborhood groups, and garage sale listing sites.
Leftover pickupWhether a local charity offers a free pickup or you need a hauler.Line up a donation option early and share it with residents before sale day.
Common area useWhether you use a clubhouse, pool lot, or park, and any cleanup.Keep the sale at individual homes unless your city or documents call for a common area.
Organizer time or feeA volunteer's hours over about 8 weeks, or a fee if you hire help.Use a checklist and ready templates, or get a quote from an organizer and compare.

An example of a city permit fee

Permit rules vary widely. As one real example, the City of San Antonio, Texas requires a permit for garage, yard, and estate sales within city limits and lists a $20 permit fee on its website, checked November 10, 2026. It also says a community garage sale for an apartment complex or HOA group must be held in a common area. Your city may have no permit at all, or very different rules.

Who usually pays

Boards handle this in different ways, and the right answer depends on your budget and your governing documents:

  • The HOA budget covers signs and printing as a community event.
  • A small participation fee from homes that sign up covers shared costs. If you go this way, say so clearly on the sign up form.
  • A sponsor, such as a local business, covers signs or printing in exchange for a mention, if your board is comfortable with that.

Questions we hear

What does a community garage sale cost an HOA?

It depends on community size, signs, printing, permit fees, promotion, and leftover pickup. We do not publish a typical total because it varies so much.

Why don't you list a price?

Every community is different, so we quote each sale after we learn about yours. Send a request and we will send you a quote.

Can residents share the cost?

Some boards ask homes that sign up for a small fee to cover shared costs. Whether that fits is up to your board and your governing documents.
