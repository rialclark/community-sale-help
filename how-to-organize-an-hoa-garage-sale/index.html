How to organize an HOA garage sale, step by step

Residents love a community sale. The hard part is that somebody has to organize it. Here is how a board or volunteer can run one from start to finish.

The steps

1. Get board approval and pick a date

Put the sale on the board's agenda, confirm your governing documents allow it, and pick a Saturday about 8 weeks out. Choose a rain date at the same meeting. Avoid holiday weekends and dates that clash with big community events.

2. Set the ground rules

Decide the sale hours, which streets or sections are included, where shoppers can park, whether common areas will be used, and where signs can go inside the community. Check your city or county rules too, since some require permits for garage sales or limit where signs can go.

3. Open resident signups

Use a short online form: name, address, email, and what they plan to sell. Signing up should be optional for every household. Only homes that sign up go on the map.

4. Tell residents, more than once

Use every channel the HOA already has: the newsletter, email list, resident portal, community boards, and neighborhood groups. A flyer at front doors helps reach people who do not read email. Do not put flyers inside mailboxes, since the U.S. Postal Service says mailboxes are only for postage paid mail. Send a reminder before signups close.

5. Build the map

Put every participating address on one shared map, plus a printable version. Google My Maps can import addresses from a spreadsheet or Google Sheet, so a form that saves to a sheet saves a lot of typing.

6. Promote the sale to shoppers

About two weeks out, post the sale in local buy and sell groups, neighborhood groups, and garage sale listing sites, with the date, hours, and map link. Put signs at community entrances and corners where your rules allow.

7. Run sale day

Share the map early that morning, check that signs are up, and have one person residents can call or text with questions.

8. Wrap up

Take signs down when the sale ends. Point residents to a donation option for leftovers, or arrange a group pickup if a local charity offers one. Send a short thank you and ask residents what to change next year.

When it makes sense to get help

Organizing usually lands on one volunteer, and it takes steady work over about 8 weeks. Getting help makes sense when nobody on the board has the time, when last year's sale fizzled because signups or promotion slipped, or when the community is large enough that the map and signups get heavy. Questions we hear

How far ahead should an HOA plan a garage sale?

About 8 weeks. Start earlier if your board only meets monthly or your city requires permits.

Does every home have to take part?

No. Signing up is up to each household. Only the homes that sign up go on the map.

Do we need a city permit?

It depends on your city or county. Some require a permit for each home, some have a community sale option, and some require nothing. Check with your city before you announce the sale.

What should we do with leftover items?

Decide before sale day. Many boards point residents to a local donation option or arrange a group pickup with a charity that offers one.
